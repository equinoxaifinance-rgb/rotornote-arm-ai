# Benchmarks and evidence gates

RotorNote compares two executable implementations of the same 48→4 real-data classifier:

- FP32 weights with scalar JavaScript dot products;
- row-wise INT8 weights with dynamic INT8 inputs and WebAssembly SIMD dot products.

The benchmark uses feature vectors extracted from the four attributed physical demo recordings. CSV parsing and FFT extraction are excluded because they are identical across engines.

```bash
npm run benchmark -- --output benchmark/results/local-x64-real.json --repetitions 51 --batch 2048 --warmups 16
```

The JSON records raw alternating-order samples, checksums, medians, p95, paired speed ratios, a deterministic 10,000-resample confidence interval, machine identity, and artifact hashes.

## Current evidence

The model artifact reduction is deterministic: FP32 is 784 bytes and INT8 is 208 bytes, a 73.4694% reduction. Across all 2,000 real four-channel recording representations, build-time parity is 100% by production label; p99 probability drift is 2.80e-10 and the disclosed maximum isolated drift is 0.01996.

Runs 31693588831, 31693809937, and 31694629861 measured identical FP32, INT8, and WASM hashes on native Arm64 Neoverse-N2 runners under Node 22.18.0. Each used 51 alternating-order samples of 2,048 inferences. Their paired-median speedups were **3.2164×**, **3.2819×**, and **3.2805×**, with deterministic bootstrap 95% intervals of **3.1751×–3.2539×**, **3.2512×–3.3015×**, and **3.2361×–3.3111×**. All three preserved 100% label agreement on the benchmark feature bank.

The separate compiled `vdotq_s32` witnesses matched the scalar result exactly. Their kernel-only ratios are not presented as product throughput. The [first green run](https://github.com/equinoxaifinance-rgb/rotornote-arm-ai/actions/runs/31693588831) downloaded artifact hashes to `ed850166408bc5f28b09726a07703b0613ca4e7deca71d813a0d6a728bf5bb4a`; the [repeat](https://github.com/equinoxaifinance-rgb/rotornote-arm-ai/actions/runs/31693809937) hashes to `cfb8d34c13bddef0d68a0e34566c276bc261a8010091e78473000193e1808816`. Their FP32, INT8, and WASM hashes are identical.

Earlier receipts under `receipts/native-arm64/` belong to superseded model artifacts. They preserve engineering history but are not current product evidence; runs 31693588831, 31693809937, and 31694629861 are the repeatability set for the current optimized artifacts.

## Native Arm gate

The workflow requires `aarch64`, rebuilds generated bytes, runs the full test/validation suite, captures raw benchmark data, and executes the NEON `vdotq_s32` witness against an exact scalar result. A native speed claim is green only when the current commit's paired confidence interval excludes 1. Energy remains unmeasured, so RotorNote makes no energy claim.

## Deeper anomaly-head benchmark

`npm run benchmark:anomaly` measures the FP32-versus-INT8 boundary for the 48->128->64->2 network. Its deterministic artifact result is 58,632 versus 15,240 learned bytes (74.0074% reduction), 100% label agreement over the complete 2,925-signal source bank, 0.00050 p99 probability drift, and 0.00586 maximum drift. Local x64 timing is diagnostic only. The native workflow emits a separate receipt and gates native execution, exact label parity, less than 0.08 maximum drift, and at least 70% learned-byte reduction; it does not substitute this timing for the linear-head speed claim.
